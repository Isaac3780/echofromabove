HANNAHThe Mother Who Prayed

Hannah: The Mother Who Prayed in Sorrow and Kept a Vow

Theory Report · Hannah, the Mother of Samuel

Hannah is one of the most beloved women in the Old Testament. She is remembered not because she was wealthy or powerful, but because of the way she prayed. Her story is recorded in 1 Samuel 1 and 2.

Hannah faced deep sorrow: she was barren in a time and culture where bearing children was considered one of the greatest blessings. Yet she did not allow her sorrow to turn her away from God. She brought her pain to Him honestly, and God answered her.

Her life teaches every woman that prayer is not only for the strong, the confident, or the self-assured. It is also for the broken-hearted who bring everything to God and trust Him with the answer.

HANNAH2
HANNAHHer Family

Hannah’s Family

Hannah was married to a man named Elkanah, who loved her. But her household also carried a painful situation.

“He had two wives. The name of the one was Hannah, and the name of the other, Peninnah. And Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children.”1 Samuel 1:2

Every year the family went up to worship and sacrifice at Shiloh. Yet each year, the pain of her situation grew.

  • Hannah had a husband who loved her.
  • Hannah had a home and a place in the family.
  • But Hannah did not have the child her heart longed for.

She shows us that a person can be loved and yet still carry deep, private sorrow.

HANNAH3
HANNAHHer Sorrow

Hannah’s Deep Sorrow

The one who provoked Hannah’s pain was Peninnah, who had children. Scripture records her cruelty plainly.

“And her rival used to provoke her grievously to irritate her, because the LORD had closed her womb.”1 Samuel 1:6

Year after year, this grief multiplied. It joined with the shame of being childless.

“Therefore she wept and would not eat.”1 Samuel 1:7

Elkanah tried to comfort her:

“Hannah, why do you weep? And why do you not eat? And why is your heart sad? Am I not more to you than ten sons?”1 Samuel 1:8

Hannah’s sorrow was real, and it was private. God’s people are not always cheerful. Our tears are known to God.

HANNAH4
HANNAHHer Prayer

In Bitterness of Soul She Prayed

Hannah did one of the most important things a person can do with their deepest pain: she brought it to God.

“She was deeply distressed and prayed to the LORD and wept bitterly.”1 Samuel 1:10

Her prayer was raw, honest, and full of faith. She made a vow before God.

“And she vowed a vow and said, ‘O LORD of hosts, if you will indeed look on the affliction of your servant and remember me and not forget your servant, but will give to your servant a son, then I will give him to the LORD all the days of his life, and no razor shall touch his head.’”1 Samuel 1:11

Notice what Hannah asked, and what she promised in return. She did not simply want a child for herself. She wanted a child she could give back to God.

HANNAH5
HANNAHMisunderstood in Prayer

Eli Misunderstood Her

Hannah prayed so intensely that her lips moved without a sound. The priest Eli watched and assumed something wrong.

“As she continued praying before the LORD, Eli observed her mouth. Hannah was speaking in her heart; only her lips moved, and her voice was not heard. Therefore Eli took her to be a drunken woman.”1 Samuel 1:12-13

Instead of growing angry, Hannah answered with honesty and dignity.

“No, my lord, I am a woman troubled in spirit. I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but I have been pouring out my soul before the LORD.”1 Samuel 1:15

Hannah teaches us that sincere prayer may look strange to others. What matters is not how people interpret our devotion, but what God sees in our hearts.

HANNAH6
HANNAHGod Answered

The Peace Before the Answer

Eli blessed her and said:

“Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition that you have made to him.”1 Samuel 1:17

Then something remarkable happened. Hannah left the place of prayer changed.

“And she said, ‘Let your servant find favour in your eyes.’ Then the woman went her way and ate, and her face was no longer sad.”1 Samuel 1:18

Hannah’s circumstances had not changed. She did not yet hold a child in her arms. But she had entrusted the whole burden to God, and peace came before the answer did.

This is a deep lesson of faith: we do not need to see the answer before we trust the One who answers.

HANNAH7
HANNAHSamuel Was Born

God Remembered Hannah

In due time, God honoured Hannah’s prayer and her vow.

“And the LORD visited Hannah, and she conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Samuel, for she said, ‘I have asked for him from the LORD.’”1 Samuel 1:20

The name Samuel means “heard of God” or “asked of God.” Every time she called his name, she would remember that God had heard her.

  • God heard the prayer of a sorrowful woman.
  • God answered and gave her a son.
  • God did not forget the vow of her heart.

When the child was weaned, Hannah kept her promise and brought Samuel to the house of the LORD at Shiloh.

HANNAH8
HANNAHA Vow Kept

Hannah Gave Samuel to the LORD

It would have been natural for Hannah to want to keep Samuel after longing for him for so long. Instead, she did exactly what she had promised.

“And when she had weaned him, she took him up with her to Shiloh. Then she said, ‘Oh, my lord! As your soul lives, my lord, I am the woman who was standing here in your presence, praying to the LORD. For this child I prayed, and the LORD has granted me my petition that I made to him. Therefore I have lent him to the LORD. As long as he lives, he is lent to the LORD.’”1 Samuel 1:24-28

Hannah’s deepest longing was not simply to have a son. It was to be faithful to the God who had heard her. She gave back freely what God had given.

Her life teaches us that answered prayer is not permission to forget the vow. It is an invitation to remain faithful.

HANNAH9
HANNAHHer Song of Praise

Hannah’s Song

Hannah’s prayer of praise is recorded in 1 Samuel 2. In it she rejoiced in who God is.

“My heart exults in the LORD; my horn is exalted in the LORD. My mouth derides my enemies, because I rejoice in your salvation. There is none holy like the LORD; there is none besides you; there is no rock like our God.”1 Samuel 2:1-2

Her song is not only about her own story. It celebrates a God who raises the lowly, feeds the hungry, and cares for the helpless.

“The LORD kills and brings to life; he brings down to Sheol and raises up. The LORD makes poor and makes rich; he brings low and he exalts.”1 Samuel 2:6-7

Hannah recognised that her personal answer was part of a much larger truth: God alone rules over every situation, and He is worthy of praise.

HANNAH10
HANNAHTrusting God’s Timing

God Blessed Her Again

After Hannah gave Samuel to the LORD, her sorrow was replaced with blessing.

“And the LORD visited Hannah, and she conceived and bore three sons and two daughters. And the boy Samuel grew in the presence of the LORD.”1 Samuel 2:21

God honoured Hannah’s obedience and gave her more children. Samuel, the son she had dedicated, grew up in the presence of the LORD and became one of the greatest prophets in Israel.

Hannah’s life shows that what we surrender to God is never wasted. Her one obedient act of faith shaped the history of God’s people for generations.

HANNAH11
HANNAHLessons From Hannah

What Hannah Teaches Women Today

  • Bring your deepest sorrows to God in honest, heartfelt prayer.
  • God hears prayers that others may misunderstand or doubt.
  • Trust God with the burden even before you see the answer.
  • Keep the vows you make to God when He answers.
  • Surrender your greatest blessings back to God’s purposes.
  • Let your answered prayers become songs of praise to God.
  • Prayer is not about perfect words; it is about a poured-out heart.

Hannah did not hide her pain. She poured out her soul before the LORD, and He heard her. Her story gives hope to every woman who waits and prays.

HANNAH12
HANNAHThe Conclusion

Hannah: A Mother Whose Prayer Changed a Nation

Hannah began her story weeping. She could not eat. She was provoked and misunderstood. But she did one thing that changed everything: she turned to God.

Through her prayer, God gave Israel a child who would hear His voice, anoint its first king, and then anoint David as king of God’s own choosing. The story of the kingdom of Israel flows through the faithfulness of this praying mother.

“And she said, ‘Let your servant find favour in your eyes.’ Then the woman went her way and ate, and her face was no longer sad.”1 Samuel 1:18

Hannah teaches the whole church that the most persistent power in this world is a heart that prays, trusts, and keeps its word to God.

“POUR OUT YOUR HEART TO GOD. HE HEARS, HE ANSWERS, AND HE NEVER FORGETS A VOW.”

HANNAH13

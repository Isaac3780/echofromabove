Noah: A Man Who Walked With God in a Corrupt Generation

Theory Report · Noah, the Man Who Built the Ark

The story of Noah is one of the most significant accounts in the book of Genesis. Noah lived during a time when human wickedness had become widespread, violence filled the earth, and people’s thoughts were continually directed toward evil.

Yet, in the midst of such a generation, Noah was described as a righteous man who walked with God. His story is therefore not only about the ark and the flood. It is a story about faith, obedience, perseverance, family, judgment, grace, worship, human weakness, and God’s continued purpose.

The central message of Noah’s life can therefore be summarized as follows:

“WHEN THE WORLD WALKED AWAY FROM GOD, NOAH CHOSE TO WALK WITH GOD.”

NOAH2
NOAHHis Family

Noah’s Birth and Family

Noah was born into a world that had already been deeply affected by sin. Humanity was experiencing the consequences of the fall, and the earth was becoming increasingly corrupt.

Yet God continued His purposes through Noah’s family. Noah eventually had three sons: Shem, Ham, and Japheth. Scripture does not give the name of Noah’s wife, but she and the wives of his three sons entered the ark with him.

Thus, Noah’s household consisted of eight people who were preserved through the flood — eight lives who walked through the world’s greatest judgment in the place of God’s salvation.

A faithful life is rarely lived alone. Noah’s obedience became the salvation of his whole household.

NOAH3
NOAHA Corrupt Generation

Noah Lived in a Corrupt Generation

Genesis gives a very serious description of Noah’s generation:

“The LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually.”Genesis 6:5

The earth was also described as corrupt and filled with violence. In a striking use of words, even “the earth was filled with violence through them.”

  • Humanity had moved far away from God’s purposes.
  • Evil had filled the earth and every intention of the heart.
  • The whole generation had turned from God.

Into this darkness, Genesis introduces a powerful contrast — and one man.

NOAH4
NOAHFavour With God

Noah Found Favour and Walked With God

In the middle of a corrupt generation, Scripture makes a dramatic turn.

“But Noah found favour in the eyes of the LORD.”Genesis 6:8

Scripture then describes what made Noah different:

“Noah was a righteous man, blameless in his generation. Noah walked with God.”Genesis 6:9

The phrase “walked with God” is one of the most important descriptions of Noah. It indicates that Noah’s relationship with God was not merely religious knowledge. His faith shaped the way he lived.

Noah was surrounded by wickedness, but he did not allow the culture around him to determine his relationship with God.

NOAH5
NOAHHis Calling

God Called Noah to Build an Ark

God’s calling came at a time when judgment was approaching.

“And God said to Noah, ‘I have determined to make an end of all flesh, for the earth is filled with violence through them. Make yourself an ark of gopher wood.’”Genesis 6:13-14

God gave Noah detailed instructions concerning the construction, dimensions, rooms, and covering of the ark. The ark was not Noah’s own invention. It was God’s instruction.

Noah had to trust God’s Word even though the event God described had not yet happened. He was called to prepare for judgment and salvation that no one could see coming.

Obedience often begins as faith in something not yet visible.

NOAH6
NOAHWhy the Ark

Judgment and Salvation

The ark was built because God was going to judge the earth through a great flood.

“For behold, I will bring a flood of waters upon the earth to destroy all flesh.”Genesis 6:17

At the same time, God established a covenant with Noah:

“But I will establish my covenant with you, and you shall come into the ark, you, your sons, your wife, and your sons’ wives with you.”Genesis 6:18

In one event, God revealed both judgment and salvation.

The flood demonstrated God’s judgment against human wickedness, while the ark became God’s appointed means of preservation for Noah and his household.

Noah did not build the ark because he was afraid of what people might think. He built it because God had spoken.

NOAH7
NOAHHis Obedience

Noah Did All That God Commanded

One of the strongest statements about Noah is found in Genesis 6:22:

“Noah did this; he did all that God commanded him.”Genesis 6:22

The same emphasis appears again when Noah prepared to enter the ark:

“And Noah did all that the LORD had commanded him.”Genesis 7:5

The repeated description demonstrates the character of Noah’s faith. He did not merely hear God’s instructions. He acted upon them.

Noah’s obedience was detailed, practical, and costly. He had to construct an enormous ark according to God’s instructions and continue doing so while the surrounding world remained unchanged. Faith that only talks is cheap; faith that obeys is costly.

NOAH8
NOAHHe Had to Wait

Obedience Includes Waiting

After Noah completed the ark, he still had to wait for God’s timing.

“And after seven days the waters of the flood came upon the earth.”Genesis 7:10

The fulfilment of God’s word came — but it came in God’s time, not Noah’s.

This is an important part of faith. Sometimes God gives an instruction, but the result does not appear immediately. Noah and his family waited inside the ark while the world outside continued its normal life.

Noah teaches us that obedience is not only about doing something. It is also about waiting.

Faith stays in the ark until God opens the door.

NOAH9
NOAHThe Flood Came

The Judgment God Announced Became Reality

Eventually, the flood came exactly as God had said. The judgment that God had announced became reality.

“Only Noah was left, and those who were with him in the ark.”Genesis 7:23

Noah, his family, and the animals preserved in the ark survived because God had provided a way of salvation.

The world that had mocked the warning was swept away, but the Word that Noah believed was shown to be true.

This reminds us that God’s warnings should never be treated casually. What God says will happen, happens.

In judgment, God remembered mercy — because He had a righteous man with a faithful family inside the ark.

NOAH10
NOAHGod Remembered Noah

“But God Remembered Noah”

After the flood had covered the earth, Genesis gives us one of the most encouraging statements in Noah’s story:

“But God remembered Noah.”Genesis 8:1

This does not mean that God had literally forgotten Noah and suddenly remembered him. Rather, it communicates God’s continuing attention and faithfulness toward Noah.

God caused a wind to pass over the earth, and the waters began to decrease.

Noah had spent a long period inside the ark, but God had not abandoned him. Months passed, and the water slowly went down. All through that time, God was watching over His servant.

Even in the long seasons of waiting, God never forgets His people.

NOAH11
NOAHLeaving the Ark

He Left by God’s Command and Built an Altar

Noah did not simply leave the ark because he personally thought the time was right. God eventually said, “Go out from the ark.” Noah had entered the ark according to God’s command, and he left according to God’s command.

When Noah finally left the ark, his first recorded response was worship.

“Then Noah built an altar to the LORD.”Genesis 8:20

After experiencing God’s preservation through the flood, Noah worshiped God.

Deliverance always deserves worship. Noah did not step out of the ark and immediately begin rebuilding his own life. His first act was worship, placing his deliverance at the centre of his relationship with God.

NOAH12
NOAHThe Covenant and the Rainbow

God’s Promise and the Rainbow

After Noah’s worship, God established a covenant with Noah and his descendants. God promised that He would never again destroy all flesh through a flood.

“I have set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and the earth.”Genesis 9:13

The rainbow became a visible reminder of God’s covenant faithfulness.

The story therefore moves from judgment to covenant, reminding us that God is not only a God of judgment but also a God who establishes and keeps His promises.

Every rainbow still speaks of a God who keeps covenant. When we see the bow in the clouds, we see grace remembered.

NOAH13
NOAHNoah’s Failure

Scripture Does Not Hide His Weakness

Noah’s story does not end with his obedience. Genesis honestly records Noah’s failure.

“Noah began to be a man of the soil, and he planted a vineyard. He drank of the wine and became drunk and lay uncovered in his tent.”Genesis 9:20-21

After seeing God’s judgments and mercies on such a scale, Noah still became vulnerable to failure.

His story gives us a serious warning: spiritual victory in one season does not mean we can become careless in another. The same gospel that saves us calls us to remain watchful until the end.

Noah remained a man of God, but he was never a perfect man. Faithfulness does not mean perfection.

NOAH14
NOAHRemain Watchful

Ham’s Response and the Warning

When Noah lay uncovered in his tent, Ham, the father of Canaan, saw his father’s nakedness and went outside and told his two brothers. Shem and Japheth responded differently. They took a garment, walked backward, and covered their father without looking at him.

When Noah awoke and learned what had happened, he pronounced words concerning his sons and their descendants, beginning: “Cursed be Canaan; a servant of servants shall he be to his brothers.”

The way we respond to another person’s weakness matters. When someone falls, do not celebrate their shame; respond with humility, dignity, and grace.

Paul later gave a similar warning to every believer:

“Therefore let anyone who thinks that he stands take heed lest he fall.”1 Corinthians 10:12
NOAH15
NOAHThe Conclusion

Noah: A Man Who Walked With God

God called Noah to build an ark because judgment was coming upon the earth. Noah believed God even though the coming flood was something he could not yet see. He obeyed God’s instructions, built the ark, entered it with his family, and waited for God’s timing.

When the flood came, God preserved Noah. When the waters finally disappeared, Noah left the ark and built an altar to worship God. God then established His covenant with Noah and gave the rainbow as a sign of His promise.

But Noah’s story also contains failure. Scripture does not hide this weakness. His story reminds us that faithful people are still human beings who need God’s grace.

“By faith Noah, being warned by God concerning events as yet unseen, in reverent fear constructed an ark for the saving of his household.”Hebrews 11:7

Be like Noah: walk with God when the world walks away. Build your faith before the rain comes.

JONAHThe One Chosen

Jonah: The One Chosen Despite His Choice to Run

Theory Report · Jonah, the Prophet Who Learned Mercy

Jonah is one of the most human prophets in Scripture. He is also one of the most reluctant. When God called him to preach to Nineveh, the great enemy city, Jonah ran in the opposite direction.

Yet running did not free Jonah. Wherever he went, God pursued him with patient, relentless love — through a storm, a great fish, a withered plant, and finally a lesson about mercy.

The book of Jonah is not only about a man and a fish. It is about a God whose mercy is wider than our prejudice and stronger than our disobedience.

JONAH2
JONAHThe Calling

God Called Jonah

The book begins with a clear, direct call from God.

“Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, ‘Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it, for their evil has come up before me.’”Jonah 1:1-2

Nineveh was the capital of Assyria, the empire that would one day threaten Israel itself. To Jonah, Nineveh was not a mission field; it was an enemy.

God’s calling did not ask Jonah what he felt about it. God simply said: arise and go. And Jonah did the exact opposite.

Jonah knew God as a God of grace — and that was precisely what made him run. He was afraid God would forgive his nation’s enemies.

JONAH3
JONAHRunning From God

Jonah Fled to Tarshish

Instead of going east to Nineveh, Jonah bought a ticket west to Tarshish — as far as he could go in the other direction.

“But Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD. He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish. So he paid the fare and went on board, to go with them to Tarshish, away from the presence of the LORD.”Jonah 1:3

Notice how deliberate his disobedience was. It was not laziness or forgetfulness. Jonah paid for the trip. He made a plan. He ran.

We can imagine a way to run from God’s call just as easily — filling our lives with other plans, other cities, other goals — but the presence of God is not a place we can escape.

JONAH4
JONAHThe Great Storm

God Sent a Great Wind

God did not argue with Jonah; He simply redirected him.

“But the LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, and there was a mighty tempest on the sea, so that the ship threatened to break up.”Jonah 1:4

While the sailors prayed to their gods and threw cargo into the sea, Jonah went below deck and fell into a deep sleep.

The captain found him and said:

“What do you mean, you sleeper? Arise, call out to your god!”Jonah 1:6

What a contrast! God had called Jonah to speak to a city, and now a pagan captain was calling Jonah to pray. Our disobedience affects more than ourselves — the people around us suffer in the storms we run into.

JONAH5
JONAHThe Sailors Cast Lots

Jonah Confessed His God

The sailors cast lots to find out who was causing the disaster, and the lot fell on Jonah. He finally confessed.

“And he said to them, ‘I am a Hebrew, and I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land.’”Jonah 1:9

The sailors asked him what they should do, and Jonah gave them an answer that is hard to hear.

“And he said to them, ‘Pick me up and hurl me into the sea, then the sea will quiet down for you, for I know it is because of me that this great tempest has come upon you.’”Jonah 1:12

Jonah preferred death over God’s call. The sailors, meanwhile, did not want to harm him, but the sea grew worse, and at last they threw him overboard — and immediately the sea ceased its raging.

JONAH6
JONAHThe Great Fish

Swallowed Alive

When Jonah’s disobedience had taken him to the point of drowning, God’s mercy met him in the deep.

“And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.”Jonah 1:17

The fish was not punishment — it was rescue. God used it to save Jonah from the death he had chosen.

Jesus connected His own death and resurrection to this event, saying He would be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth, just as Jonah was in the belly of the fish.

Even in the place of our greatest failure, God is able to preserve us for His purposes.

JONAH7
JONAHJonah’s Prayer

Out of the Belly of the Deep

Inside the fish, Jonah prayed. For the first time since his running began, he looked back to God.

“Then Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish, saying, ‘I called out to the LORD, out of my distress, and he answered me; out of the belly of Sheol I cried, and you heard my voice.’”Jonah 2:1-2

In the darkness, Jonah remembered. God had reached all the way down into the deep to hear him. At the centre of his prayer sat the truth Jonah had known all along.

“Salvation belongs to the LORD!”Jonah 2:9

Salvation belongs to God — for Israel, and for anyone God chooses to save, even Nineveh.

JONAH8
JONAHA Second Chance

God Called Jonah Again

After three days and nights, the fish released Jonah onto dry land. And then God gave him the same command again.

“Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, ‘Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you.’”Jonah 3:1-2

God did not say, “You had your chance.” The God of second chances simply reissued the call. And this time Jonah obeyed.

“So Jonah arose and went to Nineveh, according to the word of the LORD.”Jonah 3:3

Running had not changed God’s calling. It had only wasted Jonah’s time. God’s purposes do not change because we run; they simply wait for us to come back.

JONAH9
JONAHNineveh Repented

The City Heard and Believed

Jonah walked through the great city, and his message was short and sharp.

“Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!”Jonah 3:4

What happened next is one of the greatest revivals in Scripture. The people of Nineveh believed God and proclaimed a fast. Even the king removed his robes, covered himself in sackcloth, and called everyone to turn from evil.

God saw their repentance and responded with mercy.

“When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, God relented of the disaster that he had said he would do to them, and he did not do it.”Jonah 3:10

Nineveh was saved — not because Jonah was perfect, but because God is merciful and the city genuinely turned to Him.

JONAH10
JONAHAngry at Grace

The Lesson of the Plant

The story could have ended as the greatest joy of Jonah’s life. Instead, Jonah was angry because God’s mercy had fallen on Nineveh.

“But it displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was angry.”Jonah 4:1

God made a plant grow up to give Jonah shade, and then He made a worm destroy it. When Jonah grieved over the plant, God turned his grief into a living parable.

“And the LORD said, ‘You pity the plant, for which you did not labour... And should not I pity Nineveh, that great city?’”Jonah 4:10-11

Jonah cared about a plant he did not make, but not about a city of more than a hundred and twenty thousand people that God had made. God’s mercy was far wider than Jonah’s grudges.

JONAH11
JONAHThe Conclusion

Jonah: The One God Refused to Give Up On

Jonah ran from God, and God pursued him. Jonah refused to obey, and God gave him a second command. Jonah hated the people God loved, and God patiently taught him mercy.

The book of Jonah ends with God’s question hanging in the air: “Should not I pity Nineveh?” The reader is the one left to answer.

  • God’s calling does not change because we run from it.
  • God’s mercy is greater than our resistance.
  • Our obedience may be reluctant — God can still use it.
  • God’s heart reaches far beyond the people we choose to love.
  • Salvation belongs to the LORD — to Him, not to us.
“Salvation belongs to the LORD!”Jonah 2:9

“YOU CANNOT RUN FAR ENOUGH TO ESCAPE GOD’S LOVE.”
